## Synthesis
**Paragraph 1 — Points of agreement**

Both papers share a common grounding in the premise that digital technologies have fundamentally transformed their respective domains. The first paper emphasizes how the internet and social media have reshaped consumer behavior and forced companies to rethink their marketing strategies, while the second focuses on how technological advances are altering the nature of work, employment, and organizational structures. They agree that the pace of technological change is rapid and that traditional frameworks—whether in marketing scholarship or organizational psychology—are struggling to keep up. Both also implicitly recognize that researchers in their fields must continuously update their assumptions to remain relevant, and that the implications of digitalization extend well beyond any single discipline, affecting relationships (between firms and consumers in one case, between employers and employees in the other).

**Paragraph 2 — Points of disagreement or divergence**

The papers diverge significantly in their analytical focus and the locus of impact they prioritize. The marketing paper centers on the *external-facing* implications of digital technology—how firms engage with consumers through social media, data-driven targeting, and online branding—treating the organization largely as an actor adapting to a changed marketplace. The organizational behavior paper, by contrast, looks *inward and at the worker level*, examining how technology reshapes job tasks, employee experience, skill demands, and the psychological contract between workers and organizations. There is also a subtle disagreement in framing change: the marketing paper tends to portray digitalization as an opportunity-laden frontier, whereas the technology-and-work paper appears more concerned with disruption, uncertainty, and the uneven consequences of technological adoption for workers and organizational design.
