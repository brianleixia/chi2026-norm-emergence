## Synthesis
**1. Areas of Agreement**

Both papers converge on the view that digital technology is fundamentally reshaping the landscape of business and organizational life, demanding that scholarship move beyond established frameworks to address novel realities. The "Future of Digital and Social Media Marketing Research" piece emphasizes that the internet and social media have irrevocably altered consumer behavior and corporate practices, positioning digital fluency as a prerequisite for competitive relevance. Similarly, "How Technology Is Changing Work and Organizations" acknowledges that rapid technological advances have made questions of work transformation highly salient for organizational scholars. Both implicitly endorse a research agenda oriented toward adaptation: the former calls on marketing researchers to set new directions for understanding digital consumers, while the latter urges organizational psychologists to grapple with evolving employment structures. They share an underlying assumption that incremental, theory-conservative approaches are insufficient given the pace of technological change.

**2. Areas of Disagreement or Divergence**

The two papers diverge primarily in their analytical focus and the human consequences they foreground. The marketing research paper centers on strategic and commercial implications—how firms can capitalize on shifting consumer behavior through digital channels—framing technology largely as an opportunity for organizational value creation. The work and organizations paper, by contrast, foregrounds the consequences for workers and labor systems, examining how technology restructures employment relationships, job content, and organizational design. This divergence reflects a more fundamental tension: the first paper implicitly treats technology as a tool that companies deploy, whereas the second treats it as a force that reshapes people, power, and institutions. Their normative orientations also appear to differ—one is oriented toward competitive advantage and market opportunity, the other toward understanding disruption to the psychological and structural fabric of work.

**3. Most Urgent Open Question**

The most pressing question bridging these two literatures is: **How should research investigate the dual reality that digital technologies simultaneously create strategic opportunities for organizations while imposing disruptive, potentially adverse consequences on workers and consumers?** Neither paper, in isolation, adequately addresses this tension.
