## Synthesis
Based on the fragments provided, here is a synthesis of the two papers:

**1. Points of Agreement**
Both papers converge on the recognition that the proliferation of autonomous AI agents operating within multi-agent systems introduces fundamentally new security and governance challenges that traditional frameworks are ill-equipped to handle. They agree that as AI agents increasingly interact with one another—both digitally and in physical/sociotechnical environments—the resulting system-level behaviors become more complex and unpredictable than the sum of their individual parts. Both works implicitly argue that securing or governing these systems requires a shift away from analyzing individual agent behavior in isolation, and toward understanding the emergent dynamics of agent-to-agent interactions, coordination protocols, and information flows.

**2. Points of Disagreement**
While the fragments reveal the papers' shared concerns, they appear to diverge in emphasis and proposed solutions. The first paper ("Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security") seems to focus on the *security threat landscape*, treating interacting AI agents as a new attack surface with novel vulnerabilities arising from inter-agent communication and delegation. The second paper ("Integrated Design and Governance of Agentic AI Systems") appears to take a broader *sociotechnical and design-oriented* approach, emphasizing adaptive governance and the integration of human stakeholders alongside LLM-powered agents. In essence, the first frames the problem as one of adversarial security in agent ecosystems, while the second frames it as a holistic governance and design challenge—though without fuller text, specific points of contention remain unclear.
