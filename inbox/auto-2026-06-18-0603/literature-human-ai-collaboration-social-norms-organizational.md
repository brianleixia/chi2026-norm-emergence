## Synthesis
Based on the partial abstracts provided, I can offer the following synthesis, though I note both excerpts are truncated and my analysis is necessarily limited:

**1. Points of Agreement**

Both papers appear to converge on the recognition that digital technologies are fundamentally reshaping core organizational and commercial activities. The first paper emphasizes how the internet and social media have transformed consumer behavior and business operations, creating new opportunities in digital and social marketing. The second focuses on how rapid technological advancement is altering work and employment, framing this as a pressing concern for organizational scholars. Together, they share an underlying assumption that technology is not merely a tool but a disruptive force restructuring relationships—whether between firms and consumers, or between organizations and workers. Both seem to call for renewed scholarly attention to these transformations.

**2. Points of Disagreement or Divergent Emphasis**

Because the abstracts are incomplete, firm disagreements cannot be fully identified, but the two papers differ in their **focal domain and unit of analysis**. The first centers on the *external-facing* dimensions of technology—marketing, consumer interaction, and customer engagement—while the second emphasizes *internal-facing* dimensions: employment, job design, and organizational structures. This suggests they operate in largely separate scholarly conversations (marketing vs. organizational psychology/behavior) and likely prioritize different stakeholders (consumers vs. workers), different success metrics (engagement/revenue vs. well-being/productivity), and different theoretical traditions. The marketing paper appears more opportunity-oriented, whereas the organizational paper seems more concerned with consequences and challenges for human workers.
