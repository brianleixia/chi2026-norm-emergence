## Synthesis
Here is a synthesis of the two papers:

**1. Areas of Agreement**

Both papers converge on the view that digital technologies are fundamentally reshaping core organizational and commercial activities. Paper 1 frames this transformation around marketing—arguing that the internet and social media have redefined consumer behavior, enabling firms to engage customers through richer, more interactive, and data-driven channels. Paper 2 adopts a broader organizational lens, contending that digital tools are altering the very nature of work tasks, employment relationships, and organizational structures. Together, they share an underlying assumption that technology is no longer a peripheral support function but a structural force that reconfigures how value is created, exchanged, and captured—whether that value takes the form of customer relationships (Paper 1) or labor and coordination (Paper 2). Both also implicitly recognize that academic research has struggled to keep pace with the speed of these changes.

**2. Areas of Disagreement**

The papers diverge most clearly in **scope, unit of analysis, and normative framing**. Paper 1 is boundary-specific, focused on the marketing function and the firm–consumer interface, and tends to emphasize the *opportunities* digital and social media create for organizations (e.g., targeting, engagement, personalization). Paper 2 zooms out to the entire employment relationship and is markedly more cautious—highlighting displacement, intensification, surveillance, and the renegotiation of worker identity and autonomy. In effect, Paper 1 treats digital technology primarily as a value-creation lever, while Paper 2 treats it as a contested arena of power and meaning. They also differ on where the research frontier lies: Paper 1 calls for more empirical and methodological innovation within marketing scholarship, whereas Paper 2 urges organizational psychology to grapple with deep conceptual questions about what “work” and “the organization” even mean in a technology-saturated environment.
