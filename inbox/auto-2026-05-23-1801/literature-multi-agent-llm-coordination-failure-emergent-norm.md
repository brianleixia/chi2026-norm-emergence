## Synthesis
**Agreement**

All three papers converge on the fundamental premise that multi-agent systems exhibit emergent behaviors that cannot be easily predicted or controlled through traditional single-agent approaches. Papers 1 and 2 explicitly address emergent phenomena—emergent language in the former and emergent behaviors more broadly in the latter—while Paper 3 demonstrates emergent coordination patterns when multiple agents collaborate on complex tasks. Each paper acknowledges that as agents interact, novel capabilities and risks arise that require dedicated research attention, suggesting a consensus that emergent properties in multi-agent systems represent a significant research frontier.

**Disagreement**

The papers diverge in their primary concerns and methodological priorities. Paper 1 adopts a survey perspective, seeking to categorize and understand emergent language as a linguistic and cognitive phenomenon within artificial intelligence.
