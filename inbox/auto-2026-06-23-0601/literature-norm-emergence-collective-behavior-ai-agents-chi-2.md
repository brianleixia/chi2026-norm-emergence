## Synthesis
# Research Synthesis: Multi-Agent AI Security

## 1. Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the recognition that the field is moving beyond single-agent AI safety toward complex **multi-agent sociotechnical systems** where humans, AI agents, and traditional infrastructure interact in unpredictable ways. They share a common diagnosis that conventional security and governance frameworks—which assume clearly bounded perimeters, identifiable principals, and stable system architectures—are poorly suited to this emerging paradigm. Both works emphasize that **adaptive, information-rich governance mechanisms** are needed to manage the opacity and dynamism of agentic AI behavior, and that interdisciplinary integration (combining technical AI safety, cybersecurity, control theory, and organizational governance) is essential. They also implicitly agree that current industry and regulatory practices lag behind the pace of agentic AI deployment, creating a widening governance gap.

## 2. Points of Disagreement

The two papers differ markedly in **scope and orientation**. The first paper (Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security) adopts a **threat-centric, adversarial posture**, treating security primarily as a problem of defending against emergent miscoordination, attacks, and unsafe interactions between autonomous agents—framing the challenge as analogous to (but distinct from) classical cybersecurity. The second paper (Integrated Design and Governance through Adaptive Information) takes a **systems-engineering posture**, emphasizing co-design, adaptive feedback, and governance-by-information across the full lifecycle of agentic systems, with less explicit focus on adversarial threats. The first highlights *failure modes and attack surfaces*; the second highlights *design principles and control mechanisms*. They also diverge on the unit of analysis: the first focuses on agent-to-agent interactions across open environments, while the second centers on integrated human-AI teams within engineered sociotechnical workflows.
