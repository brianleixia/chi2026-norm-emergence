## Synthesis
# Research Synthesis: Digital/Social Media Marketing & Technology in Workplaces

## 1. Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the recognition that digital technologies have fundamentally reshaped core human and organizational processes, rendering prior frameworks insufficient. The first paper emphasizes how the internet and social media have transformed consumer behavior and corporate marketing strategies, while the second examines how technology has reorganized work and employment structures. They share an underlying assumption that technological change is not merely incremental but represents a paradigm shift requiring new theoretical lenses. Both implicitly argue that scholars must move beyond traditional disciplinary boundaries—the marketing paper calls for interdisciplinary integration, while the organizational psychology paper highlights the need to adapt psychological and behavioral theories to technology-mediated environments. Additionally, both implicitly endorse a stakeholder-centric view: the first foregrounds evolving consumer expectations, and the second foregrounds employee experiences and well-being in tech-saturated workplaces.

## 2. Points of Disagreement

The papers diverge in scope, focus, and underlying assumptions about agency. The marketing paper is externally oriented—concerned with how firms leverage digital tools to reach and influence consumers—while the organizational psychology paper is internally oriented—concerned with how technology shapes workers, tasks, and organizational structures from within. A more substantive disagreement lies in their framing of technological determinism: the marketing paper tends to treat digital tools as opportunities that organizations can strategically harness for competitive advantage, whereas the organizational psychology paper is more cautionary, examining how technology imposes changes on workers and raises concerns about autonomy, surveillance, and displacement. They also differ on whether the human is primarily the *target* of technology (consumers in marketing) or the *subject* affected by it (employees in organizational contexts).
