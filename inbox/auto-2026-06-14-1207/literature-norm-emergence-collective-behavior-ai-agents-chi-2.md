## Synthesis
Based on the snippets provided, here is a synthesis of the two papers:

**1. Points of Agreement**
Both papers acknowledge that the increasing autonomy and integration of AI agents into multi-agent sociotechnical systems introduce security and governance challenges that traditional, static cybersecurity frameworks are ill-equipped to handle. They share a core premise that as agents begin to interact with each other and with humans across complex digital and physical environments, there is a critical need for systematic, adaptive mechanisms to manage risk and ensure safe operation. Both implicitly agree that security must be viewed as an emergent property of the interaction between agents, rather than just a feature of individual components.

**2. Points of Disagreement**
Based on the available text, the primary difference appears to be in their scope and methodological focus. The first paper ("Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security") frames the problem primarily through the lens of **security vulnerabilities and attack surfaces** created by agent-to-agent interactions, emphasizing threats that arise from these connections. The second paper ("Integrated Design and Governance of Agentic AI Systems") takes a broader **systems engineering and governance** perspective, focusing on integrating adaptive information architectures to manage the sociotechnical dynamics between humans and AI, suggesting a more holistic design philosophy rather than purely a threat-modeling one.

**3. Most Urgent Open Question**
Given the focus of the second paper on adaptive information and governance, the most urgent open question raised by this pairing seems to be: **How can we design adaptive governance architectures that can dynamically arbitrate interactions and enforce security boundaries between heterogeneous agents (both human and AI) in real-time, without stifling legitimate autonomous functionality?** The first paper highlights the dangers of unsecured multi-agent interactions, but the second paper implies that without a mechanism for dynamic governance, simply identifying vulnerabilities is insufficient to ensure systemic safety.
