## Synthesis
# Research Synthesis

## 1. Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the view that digital technologies are fundamentally reshaping core structures of commerce and labor, requiring urgent scholarly attention. They share an assumption that the pace of technological change has outstripped organizations' ability to adapt, creating tension between opportunity and disruption. Both frame technology as a *transformative force*—not merely a tool—that reconfigures consumer behavior, employee expectations, and competitive dynamics. They also agree that research must move beyond techno-determinism to examine the human, organizational, and societal consequences of these shifts, and that interdisciplinary perspectives are needed to capture the full scope of change.

## 2. Points of Divergence

The papers diverge most sharply in **locus and directionality of impact**. The digital/social marketing paper centers on *outward-facing* dynamics—how firms reach, engage, and influence consumers through digital channels—and treats technology primarily as a marketplace and communication phenomenon. The technology-and-work paper, by contrast, focuses on *inward-facing* dynamics—how digital tools reshape internal workflows, employee identities, job design, and organizational structures. They also differ in normative framing: the marketing paper leans toward opportunity-seeking (new channels, personalization, competitive advantage), while the work-and-organizations paper leans toward caution and scrutiny (surveillance, precarity, skill displacement). Finally, they draw on largely distinct intellectual traditions—consumer behavior and marketing science versus organizational psychology and labor studies.
