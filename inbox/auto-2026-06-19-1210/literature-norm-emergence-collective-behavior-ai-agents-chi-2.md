# Research Synthesis

## 1. Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the recognition that autonomous AI agents operating in multi-agent sociotechnical environments introduce security and governance challenges that cannot be addressed through conventional frameworks alone. Each work emphasizes that the interactive nature of agentic systems—where agents negotiate, delegate, and make consequential decisions on behalf of humans—creates novel attack surfaces and failure modes distinct from single-agent or traditional software vulnerabilities. Furthermore, both papers advocate for a holistic, systems-level approach to security and governance, arguing that technical safeguards must be tightly coupled with governance structures, human oversight mechanisms, and adaptive controls. They share the view that information flow management (e.g., provenance, context, and purpose specification) is foundational to ensuring safe agent behavior, and that current evaluation and assurance methods are insufficient for these emergent, dynamic agent ecosystems.

## 2. Points of Disagreement

The two papers diverge in their primary framing and proposed solutions. The first paper adopts a *threat-centric* lens, cataloging concrete adversarial attack vectors (e.g., prompt injection across agent communication channels, collusion, emergent unsafe behaviors) and arguing that the field must develop a "red-teaming" and security-testing discipline analogous to traditional penetration testing. Its focus is largely on *preventing harm* through hardening inter-agent communication. In contrast, the second paper takes a *design-centric, governance-driven* approach, treating agentic AI systems as integrated sociotechnical systems that require adaptive information architectures and continuous alignment between technical design and institutional governance. Rather than cataloging threats, it emphasizes co-design, accountability structures, and the operationalization of governance principles into system architecture. The first sees the core problem as adversarial interaction; the second sees it as the absence of integrated design governance frameworks.

## 3. Most Urgent Open Question

The most pressing unresolved question is: **How can we develop unified, empirically validated methodologies that simultaneously (a) characterize and mitigate adversarial threats in multi-agent interactions and (b) embed adaptive governance
