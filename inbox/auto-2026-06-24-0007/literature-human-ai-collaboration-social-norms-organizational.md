- Authors: Wayne F. Cascio, Ramiro Montealegre | Year: 2016 | Venue: Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior
- Novelty: ⭐⭐
- Claims:
  - Technology is rapidly advancing and reliance on it is increasing, making its impact on work and employment a highly salient topic for organizational psychology and organizational behavior scholars.

  - The paper synthesizes existing research to provide an updated understanding of how technology is changing work and organizational structures.

## Synthesis
**Paragraph 1: Points of Agreement**

Both papers converge on the recognition that technology—particularly digital platforms and internet-enabled tools—has fundamentally reshaped the landscape of business and organizational life. The first paper emphasizes how the internet and social media have transformed consumer behavior and the methods companies use to engage customers, framing digital and social marketing as a domain rich with emerging opportunities. The second paper echoes this from an organizational perspective, noting that rapid technological advances and increased reliance on digital tools are altering the very nature of work and employment. Together, they agree that this transformation is profound, ongoing, and demands scholarly attention to understand its implications for firms, workers, and consumers alike.

**Paragraph 2: Points of Disagreement**

While both papers acknowledge technological change, they diverge in scope, focus, and underlying assumptions. The first paper adopts an explicitly optimistic, opportunity-oriented stance, emphasizing the potential gains for organizations that adopt digital and social media marketing strategies—it treats the future primarily as a domain to be shaped and exploited. The second paper takes a broader, more critical view, engaging with how technology is reshaping work and organizations holistically, including the potential disruptions, tensions, and challenges faced by employees and organizational structures. The first centers the firm-consumer interface, whereas the second centers the human-technology-workplace relationship, leading to different normative conclusions about whether technological change is principally a strategic opportunity or a complex phenomenon requiring careful management of its human and organizational consequences.

**Paragraph 3: Most Urgent Open Question**

The most pressing open question emerging from these two perspectives is: **How can organizations harness the strategic opportunities of digital and social media marketing while simultaneously managing the broader human and organizational consequences of technological transformation in the workplace?** Bridging the consumer-facing optimism of digital marketing research with the critical, human-centered concerns of organizational psychology is essential. Without integrating these lenses, scholars risk producing fragmented knowledge—where firms pursue digital engagement strategies without fully understanding the downstream effects on employees, organizational culture, and the future of work itself
