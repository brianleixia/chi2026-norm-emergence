## Synthesis
Based on the limited abstracts provided, here's a synthesis:

**1. Points of Agreement**

Both papers acknowledge that digital technology is a transformative force reshaping contemporary organizational and commercial landscapes. Paper 1 emphasizes how the internet and social media have fundamentally altered consumer behavior and business practices, creating new opportunities while demanding fresh research agendas. Paper 2 similarly frames technology as a driver of structural change, this time within work and employment contexts. Both implicitly agree that scholars must develop new frameworks to keep pace with rapid technological evolution, and that organizations can no longer treat digital tools as peripheral—they are central to how value is created, exchanged, and produced.

**2. Points of Disagreement or Divergent Emphasis**

While both papers concern technology's organizational impact, they diverge in scope and primary focus. Paper 1 centers on the *external-facing* dimension: how firms reach and influence consumers through digital and social channels, foregrounding marketing strategy and customer behavior. Paper 2, by contrast, focuses on the *internal-facing* dimension: how technology restructures work itself, employment relationships, and organizational psychology. Additionally, Paper 1 appears more optimistic—framing digital marketing as offering "significant opportunities"—whereas Paper 2's framing of technology "changing work and organizations" is more neutral or cautionary, leaving room to interrogate displacement, surveillance, and worker well-being. This reflects a broader disciplinary split between marketing research's growth-orientation and organizational psychology's human-impact concerns.

**3. Most Urgent Open Question**

The most pressing unresolved question bridging both papers is: **How can organizations capture the commercial value of digital technologies without sacrificing the human dimensions of work and consumer trust?** In other words, as firms deploy increasingly sophisticated digital and social media strategies, what governance mechanisms, ethical guardrails, and organizational designs are needed to ensure that technological gains do not come at the cost of employee well-being, data privacy, or authentic consumer relationships?
