## Synthesis
1. What do they agree on?
Both papers agree that the integration of AI agents into multi-agent systems—spanning both digital and physical environments—creates security and governance challenges that existing frameworks are ill-equipped to address. They share a recognition that traditional cybersecurity and AI safety paradigms, developed primarily for single-agent or isolated systems, fail to capture the emergent risks arising when heterogeneous agents (including humans, AI systems, and organizations) interact at scale. Both works emphasize the need for interdisciplinary approaches that blend technical safeguards with governance mechanisms, and they highlight the inadequacy of purely reactive or static defenses in the face of adaptive, autonomous agents.

2. Where do they disagree?
The papers diverge in primary focus and framing. The first paper ("Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security") centers on *adversarial and threat-oriented* concerns—emphasizing attack surfaces, manipulation, collusion, and the defensive measures needed to protect systems of interacting AI agents. Its framing is closer to traditional security research, focused on identifying and mitigating specific classes of threats. The second paper ("Integrated Design and Governance of Agentic AI Systems through Adaptive Information") takes a more *constructive and systemic* view, treating governance as a co-design problem where adaptive information structures must be embedded into the system from the outset. It foregrounds alignment, transparency, and regulatory compliance as first-class design objectives rather than as defensive add-ons. In short, the first asks "how do we secure these systems?" while the second asks "how do we build them to be governable?"

3. Most urgent open question?
How can security and governance be *co-engineered* in multi-agent AI systems such that defensive mechanisms and adaptive governance structures are not treated as separate layers, but as mutually reinforcing components of a single design process—capable of responding in real time to emergent behaviors without sacrificing either robustness or legitimate autonomy?
