## 1. Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security: Towards Secure Systems of Interacting AI Agents
- Authors: Christian Schroeder de Witt, Krawiecka, Klaudia, Krawczuk, Igor | Year: 2025 | Venue: arXiv (Cornell University)
- Novelty: ⭐⭐
- Claims:
  - AI agents are beginning to interact with each other directly and across internet platforms and physical environments, creating security challenges beyond traditional cybersecurity and AI safety frameworks
  - Free-form protocols are essential for AI's task generalization but enable new threats like secret collusion and coordinated swarm attacks
  - Network effects can rapidly spread privacy breaches

## 2. Integrated Design and Governance of Agentic AI Systems through Adaptive Information Modulation
- Authors: Qiliang Chen, Sepehr Ilami, Nunzio Lorè | Year: 2024 | Venue: arXiv (Cornell University)
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐
- Claims:
  - Modern engineered systems increasingly involve complex sociotechnical environments where multiple agents, including humans and LLM-based agentic AI, must navigate social dilemmas pitting individual interests against collective welfare.
  - Engineered systems are evolving toward multi-agent architectures with autonomous LLM-based agents, rendering traditional governance approaches insufficient.
  - Agentic AI powered by large language models represents an emerging paradigm that requires integrated design and governance through adaptive information modulation.

## Synthesis
## Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the recognition that systems comprising multiple interacting agents—particularly those involving AI—exceed the analytical capacity of any single discipline or governance framework. They share a diagnosis that traditional cybersecurity, AI safety, and conventional systems engineering approaches are insufficient on their own to address the emergent risks that arise when agents operate in dynamic, interconnected environments. Both also emphasize the need for **adaptive, continuous, and context-sensitive** governance mechanisms rather than static, point-in-time controls, and they each treat the alignment between technical architecture and institutional oversight as a first-class design concern rather than an afterthought.

## Points of Disagreement

The most salient divergence is in **scope and locus of intervention**. The first paper frames the challenge primarily as a *security* problem emerging from inter-agent interaction, focusing on threat models, vulnerabilities, and defensive postures between autonomous agents across digital and physical domains. The second paper takes a broader *sociotechnical governance* perspective, positioning adaptive information governance as the integrating mechanism that links agentic AI design to organizational and regulatory structures. In other words, the first asks "how do we keep interacting agents from harming one another and their environments?" while the second asks "how do we embed governance into the design fabric of agentic systems?" The first is reactive and adversarial in orientation; the second is proactive and constitutive.

## Most Urgent Open Question

How can we develop **operational metrics and empirical benchmarks** that meaningfully capture the security and governance properties of multi-agent AI systems *before* they are widely deployed? Without such measurements, both the threat-modeling approach of the first paper and the design-time governance approach of the second remain conceptually compelling but practically unverified, leaving policymakers and engineers without the evidence base needed to choose between—let alone integrate—these two complementary framings.
