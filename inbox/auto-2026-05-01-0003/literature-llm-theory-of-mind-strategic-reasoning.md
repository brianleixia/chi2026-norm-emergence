## Synthesis

### Agreement
The HSE economics papers (Jul 2025, Dec 2025) and the arXiv beauty contest paper (Feb 2025) all agree that LLMs show distinctive behavior in game-theoretic settings. Key finding: LLMs tend to anchor on "sophisticated" rather than naive responses, overestimate human rationality, and fail at identifying dominant strategies in simple two-player games.

### Disagreement
The NeurIPS MindGames challenge suggests frontier models have measurable but variable ToM capabilities, while the HSE papers suggest fundamental limitations in strategic reasoning. The gap may be between "pattern-matching ToM" and genuine strategic reasoning.

### Open Question
**Most urgent:** Can multi-agent LLM populations develop shared strategic conventions (a form of social norm) through iterative beauty contest interactions? No paper directly addresses whether LLM agents form stable collective strategies over time — only single-shot or few-shot experiments exist.

**Implication for CHI 2026:** The ToM-strategy gap is a strong theoretical foundation for why LLM agents might fail at norm convergence. If agents can't reason about each other's reasoning, how do they coordinate on social conventions? This directly motivates the research question.
