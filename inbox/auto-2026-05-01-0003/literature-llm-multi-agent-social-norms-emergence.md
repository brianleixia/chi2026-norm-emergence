## Synthesis

### Agreement
All papers agree that social norms and coordination emerge spontaneously in populations of LLM agents without central coordination. The mechanisms are robust across different architectures and prompting strategies. Ashery et al. (2025) in Science Advances provides the strongest empirical foundation.

### Disagreement
Key tension: Does "emergence" require minimal behavioral diversity (Alvarez et al./SWARM hypothesis) or does it arise from rational strategic reasoning (Bis二层/Azizi et al.)? The over-competition paper (Sep 2025) argues competitive pressure drives pathological norms, while TerraLingua shows cooperation-dominant cultures can also emerge.

### Open Question
**Most urgent:** How do emergent LLM social norms compare quantitatively to human social norm emergence? The TST paper (Apr 2026) and Nature paper (Mar 2026) provide network-level data but no direct comparison to human social network dynamics. This is a critical gap for the "norm emergence" framing in the CHI paper.

**Implication for CHI 2026:** The Science Advances paper (Ashery et al.) is the strongest related work and should anchor the literature review. The "over-competition" failure mode (Sep 2025) directly maps to a threat scenario in our framework.
