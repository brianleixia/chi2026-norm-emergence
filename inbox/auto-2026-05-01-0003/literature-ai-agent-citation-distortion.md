## Synthesis

### Agreement
All sources agree that AI-generated hallucinated citations are a serious, widespread, and growing problem in scientific literature. NeurIPS 2025 is the most documented case so far (100+ citations across 51 papers). Nature's estimate of "tens of thousands" of affected 2025 publications suggests the problem is systemic.

### Disagreement
No real disagreement — all sources confirm the problem. Key nuance: the Taylor & Francis paper discusses the mechanism (generative AI produces plausible but fabricated references) while GPTZero/others document the empirical scope.

### Open Question
**Most urgent:** What is the rate of hallucinated citations specifically in CHI, CSCW, and HCI venues? NeurIPS is CS/ML — do HCI papers fare better or worse? This is directly relevant to our "citation distortion" threat model.

**Implication for CHI 2026:** This topic is highly timely. The arXiv taxonomy paper (Feb 2026) provides a concrete failure mode taxonomy that could be adapted for our "citation distortion" scenario in the norm emergence paper. This should be positioned as a real, documented risk, not speculation.
