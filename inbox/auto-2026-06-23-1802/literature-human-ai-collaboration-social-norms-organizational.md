## Synthesis
Here is a synthesis of the two papers based on the limited excerpts available:

## 1. Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the observation that digital technologies are fundamentally reshaping core organizational and commercial activities. The first paper emphasizes how the internet and social media have transformed consumer behavior and the ways companies conduct business, while the second focuses on how technology is altering the nature of work and employment itself. Together, they share a common framing: technology is not merely a tool supporting existing processes but a disruptive force that restructures relationships—between firms and consumers in one case, and between organizations and workers in the other. Both papers also appear to position themselves as agenda-setting contributions, signaling that research in their respective domains must evolve to keep pace with rapid technological change.

## 2. Points of Disagreement

The papers appear to differ in their primary unit of analysis and disciplinary orientation. The first centers on the **external** interface between organizations and markets—consumer behavior, marketing strategy, and competitive dynamics—treating technology as a driver of commercial transformation. The second centers on the **internal** organizational environment, focusing on employment, work design, and the psychological experience of workers. This produces different underlying assumptions: the marketing paper tends to frame technological change as an *opportunity* for organizations to reach and engage consumers in new ways, whereas the work-and-organizations paper appears more concerned with the *consequences and tensions* technology introduces for employees and organizational structures. Neither excerpt engages substantively with the other's domain, suggesting these literatures have developed in relative isolation.
