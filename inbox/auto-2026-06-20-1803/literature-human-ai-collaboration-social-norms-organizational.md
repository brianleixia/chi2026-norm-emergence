## Synthesis
**Points of Agreement**

Both papers converge on the recognition that digital technology has fundamentally reshaped core human and organizational activities, creating both opportunities and challenges that demand urgent scholarly attention. They share an understanding that the internet and social platforms have transformed traditional patterns of consumer behavior, communication, and work, disrupting established frameworks in marketing and organizational psychology respectively. Importantly, both works emphasize the need for interdisciplinary and forward-looking research approaches, arguing that existing theories may be insufficient to capture the velocity and scope of technological change. They also align on the view that technology is not a neutral tool but an agent that actively reconfigures relationships—between firms and consumers in one case, and between workers, organizations, and labor markets in the other.

**Points of Divergence**

The papers diverge in their primary domain of focus and the nature of the transformations they foreground. The digital and social media marketing paper centers on consumer-facing phenomena, examining how platforms reshape brand communication, customer engagement, and market dynamics from a firm perspective. The technology and work paper, by contrast, attends to employee and organizational dimensions, including labor displacement, skill demands, and the psychological implications of technologically mediated work. They also differ in stance: the marketing piece tends toward an opportunity-oriented framing, emphasizing the strategic potential for organizations, whereas the organizational psychology paper leans toward a more cautious examination of disruption, inequality, and adaptation challenges facing workers. Their methodological and disciplinary lenses therefore produce different priorities—one foregrounds managerial and strategic concerns, the other human and societal ones.

**Most Urgent Open Question**

The most pressing unresolved question is how to develop integrated theoretical frameworks that capture the simultaneous and intertwined transformations occurring on both the consumer and worker sides of the digital economy. As marketing platforms, automation, and AI reshape what consumers want and how firms deliver it, these same technologies are simultaneously redefining the nature of work, employment relationships, and organizational structures.
