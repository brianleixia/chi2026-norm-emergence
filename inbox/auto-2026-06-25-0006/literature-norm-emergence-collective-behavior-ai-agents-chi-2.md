# Research Synthesis

## Areas of Agreement

Both papers converge on the recognition that AI agents are no longer operating in isolation but are increasingly embedded in **multi-agent sociotechnical systems** where interaction—not individual capability—is the primary locus of risk and complexity. They share a commitment to moving beyond siloed approaches (purely technical cybersecurity on one hand, purely AI alignment on the other) toward integrated frameworks that treat security, governance, and coordination as deeply entangled concerns. Both implicitly endorse a **defense-in-depth posture**: Paper 1 frames security as requiring layered, systemic thinking across communication, computation, and coordination surfaces, while Paper 2 advances adaptive governance architectures that dynamically respond to uncertainty in agent behavior and environmental context. They also agree that **human oversight remains essential**—not as a rubber stamp, but as an active design element integrated into the agent's operational loop, particularly in high-stakes domains.

## Areas of Disagreement

The papers diverge most sharply on **the unit of analysis and the locus of intervention**. Paper 1 adopts an adversarial, threat-centric framing, treating emergent multi-agent ecosystems as an *attack surface* and emphasizing protocol-level vulnerabilities (e.g., inter-agent communication hijacking, collusion, prompt injection across agent boundaries). Paper 2, by contrast, is fundamentally **constructivist and design-oriented**, focusing on how to *build* agentic systems that are robust, interpretable, and governable from the outset—particularly in regulated, high-stakes domains like medical AI. This produces different priorities: Paper 1 privileges discovering and cataloging attack vectors; Paper 2 privileges designing information flows and governance structures that *prevent* failures before they manifest. There is also a subtle epistemic tension: Paper 1 tends to assume the threat landscape is knowable and enumeratable through red-teaming, while Paper 2 explicitly incorporates **deep uncertainty and adaptive learning** as core design constraints, suggesting that the threat space cannot be exhaustively mapped in advance.
