# Research Synthesis

## Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on a fundamental observation: the increasing integration of AI agents into complex sociotechnical systems creates governance and security challenges that traditional frameworks cannot adequately address. They share a concern that multi-agent environments—particularly those involving AI-to-AI interaction or human-AI collaboration—introduce emergent risks that require novel theoretical and practical approaches. Both also implicitly or explicitly recognize that agentic AI systems, particularly those based on large language models, represent a qualitatively different category of system than previous automated tools, necessitating adaptive rather than static governance mechanisms. Additionally, both papers appear to acknowledge that technical solutions alone are insufficient and that effective approaches must integrate considerations of information flow, trust, and systemic coordination.

## Points of Disagreement

The papers appear to differ in their primary framing and emphasis. The first paper, focused on "Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security," prioritizes the **security and adversarial dimensions** of interacting AI systems—threat modeling, vulnerabilities in agent-to-agent communication, and defensive measures against malicious actors. The second paper, centered on "Integrated Design and Governance of Agentic AI Systems," adopts a broader **sociotechnical systems perspective**, emphasizing the co-design of technical architectures and governance structures for legitimate collaborative systems. In essence, one paper treats multi-agent interaction primarily as an *attack surface to be defended*, while the other treats it as a *coordination problem to be architected*. They may also diverge on whether adaptive information architectures serve primarily as a security mechanism (constraining information flow to prevent harm) or as an enabler of effective collaboration (facilitating appropriate knowledge sharing among agents).
