## Synthesis
1. **Where they agree:** Both papers share a foundational consensus that technology—particularly digital and internet-based tools—is fundamentally reshaping the terrain of human activity in the 21st century. Paper 1 emphasizes this transformation in the consumer behavior and business strategy domain, while Paper 2 situates it within the workplace and organizational psychology context. Both agree that this shift is rapid, ongoing, and significant enough to demand dedicated scholarly attention. They also implicitly share an urgency about keeping academic research relevant and adaptive to these technological realities rather than treating them as peripheral phenomena.

2. **Where they disagree:** The papers diverge in their primary focus and disciplinary framing. Paper 1 is centered on the external-facing implications of digital and social media—how organizations reach consumers, build brands, and conduct commercial activity—treating technology largely as a marketing and communications tool. Paper 2, by contrast, focuses on the internal, human-experiential dimensions: how technology alters employment structures, worker psychology, job design, and organizational dynamics. This creates a subtle disagreement in orientation: one frames technology as a strategic lever for value creation with consumers, while the other frames it as a disruptive force affecting worker identity, well-being, and the nature of work itself. They also appear to differ on whose experience matters most—Paper 1 privileges the firm-customer interface, whereas Paper 2 privileges the employee-organization relationship.

3. **Most urgent open question:** The most pressing unresolved question bridging both papers is how the convergence of digital marketing technologies (AI-driven personalization, algorithmic targeting, social media analytics) with workplace technologies (collaboration platforms, productivity monitoring, gig-economy infrastructure) is jointly reshaping the psychological contract between individuals, organizations, and society.
