# Research Synthesis

**What They Agree On:**
The paper fundamentally agrees that social norm interventions are a valuable tool for promoting prosocial behavior change, leveraging the psychological mechanism that people are influenced by perceived prevalence of behaviors. The research recognizes that highlighting what is common or typical can shape individual conduct effectively—making this approach both cost-effective and scalable.

**Where They Disagree or Tension Exists:**
The paper identifies a critical tension: while social norm interventions assume shared social reference points, populations are often heterogeneous, meaning different groups may have divergent understandings of what constitutes "normal" or "prevalent" behavior. This heterogeneity can undermine interventions designed around uniform norms, creating a mismatch between the message being broadcast and the actual social reality experienced by diverse subgroups.
