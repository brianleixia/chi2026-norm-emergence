## Synthesis
**Points of Agreement**

Both papers converge on the recognition that digital technologies are fundamentally reshaping core human and organizational dynamics. They agree that the internet and digital platforms have moved beyond being mere operational tools to become structural forces that reconfigure consumer behavior, employee expectations, and organizational processes. Both frame technology as a catalyst for transformation rather than incremental change—Paper 1 emphasizes how social and digital media have altered consumer engagement and marketing practice, while Paper 2 emphasizes how technology is reshaping the nature of work, employment relationships, and organizational structures. They also share an implicit normative stance: both treat rapid technological change as a defining challenge that scholars and practitioners must urgently understand and respond to, and both call for research agendas that move beyond hype toward rigorous inquiry into behavioral and organizational consequences.

**Points of Disagreement or Divergence**

The two papers diverge significantly in their *level of analysis* and in the *actor* they position as central. Paper 1 foregrounds the **consumer–firm dyad**, treating digital and social media primarily as channels through which companies reach, influence, and serve increasingly empowered consumers. Its concerns center on marketing strategy, brand–consumer relationships, and how firms can capture value from digital engagement. Paper 2, by contrast, foregrounds the **worker–organization dyad**, examining how technology reshapes job design, labor markets, psychological contracts, and organizational design from the perspective of organizational psychology. A subtler divergence is normative tone: Paper 1 is largely *opportunity-oriented*, framing digital media as opening "significant opportunities" for organizations, whereas Paper 2 is more *ambivalent and cautionary*, raising questions about displacement, surveillance, and the blurring of work–nonwork boundaries. The two papers thus offer complementary but unintegrated lenses—one outward toward markets, the other inward toward employees.

**Most Urgent Open Question**

The most pressing unresolved question is **how digital transformation that simultaneously reshapes consumer behavior and the internal organization of work can be studied as a single integrated phenomenon
