## Synthesis
# Research Synthesis: Multi-Agent AI Security & Agentic AI Governance

## Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the recognition that multi-agent and agentic AI systems introduce fundamentally new security and governance challenges that traditional frameworks cannot adequately address. The first paper focuses on emergent risks arising when AI agents interact directly with one another across digital and physical environments, while the second examines the integration of agentic AI into complex sociotechnical systems alongside human actors. Both implicitly (and in their core arguments explicitly) acknowledge that the autonomy, adaptability, and inter-agent communication capabilities of modern AI agents create attack surfaces and coordination challenges that exceed what classical cybersecurity, AI safety, or single-agent governance models were designed to handle. They share a concern with how information flows, decision-making authority, and trust relationships must be rethought when agents—whether human or artificial—operate with increasing independence within interconnected ecosystems.

## Points of Disagreement

The papers diverge notably in their disciplinary orientation and proposed solutions. The first paper adopts a security-centric posture, framing the problem primarily through adversarial thinking—threat modeling, attack vectors, defensive postures, and risk mitigation across heterogeneous agent populations. It treats multi-agent interaction as a vulnerability surface requiring hardened protocols and verification mechanisms. The second paper, by contrast, emphasizes adaptive information governance and integrated design, advocating for governance structures that evolve alongside system behavior. Where the security paper asks "how do we prevent compromise?", the governance paper asks "how do we enable trustworthy coordination?"—a distinction between defensive containment and constructive stewardship. Additionally, the second paper places greater weight on human-AI teaming dynamics and the sociotechnical embedding of agentic systems, whereas the first centers AI-AI interactions as the primary locus of concern, treating human involvement as a secondary consideration.
