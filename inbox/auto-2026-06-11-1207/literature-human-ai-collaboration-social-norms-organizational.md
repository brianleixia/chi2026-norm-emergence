## 2. How Technology Is Changing Work and Organizations
- Authors: Wayne F. Cascio, Ramiro Montealegre | Year: 2016 | Venue: Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior
- Novelty: ⭐⭐
- Claims:
  - The effects of technology on work and organizations is a highly salient research question for scholars of organizational psychology and organizational behavior (OP/OB).
  - Rapid advances in technology and increased reliance on technology are driving changes in work and employment.
  - Current OP/OB research is making progress in examining the effects of technology on work and organizations, though the article reviews the direction and purpose of this work.

## Synthesis
**1. Points of Agreement**

Both papers converge on the recognition that digital technologies are fundamentally reshaping the landscape of business and work at an accelerating pace. The first paper emphasizes how internet and social media have transformed consumer behavior and organizational practices, creating significant opportunities through social and digital marketing. The second paper echoes this transformation from the organizational and employment perspective, noting that rapid technological advances are altering how work is conducted and structured. Both implicitly agree that traditional frameworks are being challenged and that scholarship must evolve to keep pace with these changes—each calling for research agendas that address emerging realities rather than relying on outdated models.

**2. Points of Disagreement or Divergence**

While the papers share a thematic concern with technological change, they differ substantially in their scope, focus, and analytical lens. The first paper is centered on the micro-level dynamics of marketing and consumer interaction, framing digital tools as opportunities for organizational engagement and value creation. The second paper adopts a broader, more critical perspective on technology's impact on work and organizations, engaging with questions of employment, labor structures, and the psychological implications for workers. Additionally, the first paper carries a more optimistic, opportunity-oriented tone, whereas the second appears more attuned to the disruptive and potentially destabilizing consequences of technological integration in workplaces.

**3. Most Urgent Open Question**

The most pressing open question emerging from these two perspectives is: **How can organizations harness digital and social technologies to create value for consumers and businesses without exacerbating inequities, job displacement, or worker well-being concerns?** Bridging the optimistic marketing view with the more cautious organizational psychology perspective is essential. Future research must move beyond siloed examinations—whether of consumer engagement or labor disruption—to develop integrated frameworks that address both the opportunities and the human costs of digital transformation in a holistic manner.
