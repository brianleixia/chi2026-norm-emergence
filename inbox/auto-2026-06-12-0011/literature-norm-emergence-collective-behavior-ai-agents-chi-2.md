# Synthesis of Two Papers on Multi-Agent AI Security and Governance

## 1. Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the recognition that the emergence of **interacting AI agents** fundamentally transforms the security and governance landscape, moving beyond single-system concerns. Paper 1 explicitly frames this as a need for "secure systems of interacting AI agents," while Paper 2 describes a "complex sociotechnical environment" populated by humans and agentic AI systems. The two works share a diagnosis that **existing frameworks are insufficient**: traditional cybersecurity, classical AI safety, and conventional systems engineering each address fragments of the problem but fail to capture emergent dynamics arising from agent-to-agent interaction. Both implicitly accept that governance and security cannot be retrofitted as an afterthought—they must be designed into systems from inception.

## 2. Points of Divergence

The papers diverge significantly in **orientation and prescription**. Paper 1 adopts a *threat-centric, adversarial posture*, cataloging attack surfaces, vulnerabilities, and the novel risks introduced when AI agents communicate directly (e.g., prompt-injection across agent boundaries, compromised coordination, identity spoofing). Its concern is largely with **defense against misuse and compromise**. Paper 2, by contrast, takes a *systems-engineering and governance posture*, proposing "adaptive information" frameworks for designing trustworthy multi-agent systems from the ground up, with humans-in-the-loop and continuous alignment. Where Paper 1 asks "how can this be attacked or subverted?", Paper 2 asks "how can this be designed responsibly?" A second divergence lies in **scope of agency**: Paper 1 focuses on autonomous agents negotiating across networks, while Paper 2 is more interested in the co-governance relationship between humans and agentic AI as collaborators within engineered workflows.
