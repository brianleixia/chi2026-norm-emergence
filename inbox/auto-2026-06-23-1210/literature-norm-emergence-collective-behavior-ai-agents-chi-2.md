## Synthesis
# Research Synthesis: Multi-Agent AI Security and Agentic AI Governance

## 1. Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the recognition that as AI systems transition from isolated, single-agent deployments to **multi-agent, sociotechnical ecosystems**, traditional safety and security paradigms are insufficient. They agree that:

- **Human-AI teaming is essential.** Neither paper advocates for fully autonomous AI governance; both emphasize the necessity of meaningful human oversight, whether through governance frameworks (Paper 1) or adaptive information-sharing mechanisms (Paper 2).
- **Governance must be dynamic, not static.** Paper 1 calls for new frameworks addressing the security challenges of interacting agents, while Paper 2 explicitly proposes *adaptive* governance—implying that fixed rule sets cannot accommodate evolving agentic behaviors.
- **Trust and coordination are foundational challenges.** Paper 1 highlights trust establishment as critical in multi-agent security, and Paper 2 positions information sharing as the central mechanism for building that trust.

## 2. Points of Disagreement

The papers diverge significantly in **scope, threat model, and methodological emphasis**:

- **Locus of risk:** Paper 1 focuses on *adversarial* risks—agents exploiting each other, impersonation, collusion, and emergent unsafe behaviors across open systems. Paper 2 is largely *cooperative* in framing, treating agents as collaborators with humans and focusing on alignment through shared information rather than defending against malicious actors.
- **System boundaries:** Paper 1 explicitly considers **open, internet-scale** interactions where agents from different parties with misaligned incentives must coexist, whereas Paper 2 appears to assume a more **bounded engineered system** (e.g., a single organization or platform) where governance structures can be designed holistically.
- **Solution type:** Paper 1 leans toward identifying *open challenges* and calling for new research, while Paper 2 proposes a concrete architectural framework (integrated design and governance via adaptive information). This reflects a theory-vs.-engineering divide.
