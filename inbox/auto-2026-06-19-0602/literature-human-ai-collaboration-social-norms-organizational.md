## Synthesis
**Paragraph 1 – Points of Agreement**

Both papers acknowledge that digital technology has fundamentally restructured core human and organizational activities, demanding fresh scholarly attention. The first paper emphasizes how the internet and social media have transformed consumer behavior and compelled firms to rethink marketing strategies, while the second frames technology as a disruptive force reshaping the nature of work and organizational life. Together, they converge on the view that digital transformation is not a marginal trend but a pervasive shift requiring researchers to update established theories, methodologies, and pedagogical approaches. Both also recognize that academic disciplines must proactively set research agendas rather than reactively chase developments, and that the rapid pace of technological change creates significant knowledge gaps between practice and scholarship.

**Paragraph 2 – Points of Disagreement**

The papers diverge in their domain of focus and in the locus of disruption they emphasize. The digital/social media marketing paper concentrates on the customer-facing side of organizations—consumer engagement, brand communication, and market strategy—whereas the technology-and-work paper centers on internal organizational dynamics such as employee experience, labor structures, and psychological contracts. They also differ on the primary actor driving change: marketing research foregrounds shifts in consumer behavior enabled by platforms, while organizational research foregrounds managerial and technological decisions altering how work is designed and performed. Finally, their normative orientations differ—the marketing paper is largely optimistic about opportunity creation, whereas the work-and-technology paper tends to be more cautious, emphasizing risks to worker well-being, identity, and employment stability.
