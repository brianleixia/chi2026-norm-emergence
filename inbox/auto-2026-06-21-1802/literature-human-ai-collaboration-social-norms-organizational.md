# Research Synthesis

## 1. Points of Agreement

Both papers converge on the view that digital technology has become a foundational force reshaping core human activities—Paper 1 emphasizes this in the consumer-marketing interface, where internet and social media have permanently altered buying behavior and business operations, while Paper 2 frames it within work and organizational life, where technology now mediates how tasks are performed, how employment relationships are structured, and how organizational psychology must be theorized. They agree that this transformation is not incremental but rapid and structural, producing effects that span individual psychology, interpersonal dynamics, and institutional arrangements. Both also implicitly call for scholarship that takes technological change as a primary object of inquiry rather than a background condition, urging researchers to update established frameworks to match a digitalized reality.

## 2. Points of Disagreement

The papers diverge most clearly in **scope and disciplinary orientation**. Paper 1 treats digital technology largely as a *commercial enabler*—its lens is on how firms leverage social and digital channels to reach consumers, build relationships, and capture value—framing the change in terms of opportunity and strategic advantage for organizations. Paper 2, by contrast, treats technology as a *disruptive force with ambivalent human consequences*, attending to risks such as job displacement, shifting skill demands, blurred work-life boundaries, surveillance, and psychological strain. Where Paper 1 implicitly assumes organizational actors remain in control of technology, Paper 2 questions that assumption, suggesting workers and organizations are often shaped by technological trajectories they did not choose. There is also a temporal disagreement: Paper 1 reads the digital shift as an evolving but manageable research frontier, whereas Paper 2 frames it as an urgent present-tense disruption already destabilizing employment and identity.

The most pressing unresolved question bridging both literatures is: **Under what conditions does digital technology augment human agency—enhancing consumer welfare, meaningful work, and organizational capability—rather than erode it through surveillance
