## Synthesis
**1. Points of Agreement**

Both papers share a common grounding in the premise that digital technologies have fundamentally reshaped organizational and consumer landscapes, demanding new theoretical and managerial perspectives. Paper 1 emphasizes how the internet and social media have transformed consumer behavior and business practices, while Paper 2 frames AI as a similarly disruptive force reshaping the management domain. Both implicitly accept that technology is no longer a peripheral tool but a structural force reconfiguring how value is created, how decisions are made, and how organizations relate to their stakeholders. They also share an agenda-setting orientation: each appears positioned not merely to describe current developments but to chart a research trajectory, suggesting both authors view the present moment as a transitional one requiring deliberate scholarly intervention.

**2. Points of Disagreement or Divergent Emphasis**

The papers diverge notably in their locus of concern and the nature of the transformation they foreground. Paper 1 centers on the *external-facing* dimension of technology—how digital and social media alter the marketer-consumer interface, with opportunities for organizations framed largely in terms of engagement, reach, and behavioral insight.
