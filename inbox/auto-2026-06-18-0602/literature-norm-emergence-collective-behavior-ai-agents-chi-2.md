## 1. Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security: Towards Secure Systems of Interacting AI Agents
- Authors: Christian Schroeder de Witt, Krawiecka, Klaudia, Krawczuk, Igor | Year: 2025 | Venue: arXiv (Cornell University)
- Novelty: ⭐⭐
- Claims:
  - AI agents are beginning to interact with each other directly and across internet platforms and physical environments
  - Free-form protocols are essential for AI's task generalization but enable new threats like secret collusion and coordinated swarm attacks
  - Network effects can rapidly spread privacy breaches

## 2. Integrated Design and Governance of Agentic AI Systems through Adaptive Information Modulation
- Authors: Qiliang Chen, Sepehr Ilami, Nunzio Lorè | Year: 2024 | Venue: arXiv (Cornell University)
- Novelty: ⭐⭐⭐
- Claims:
  - Modern engineered systems increasingly involve complex sociotechnical environments where multiple agents, including humans and agentic AI powered by large language models, must navigate social dilemmas that pit individual interests against collective welfare.
  - Traditional governance approaches are insufficient for multi-agent architectures with autonomous LLM-based agents, requiring integrated design and governance frameworks.
  - Agentic AI systems can be effectively designed and governed through adaptive information modulation to address social dilemmas in sociotechnical environments.

## Synthesis
**Areas of Agreement**

Both papers converge on the fundamental premise that as AI systems—particularly agentic and multi-agent ones—become embedded in complex sociotechnical environments, traditional security, safety, and governance frameworks are insufficient on their own. They share a recognition that the dynamic, adaptive, and interactive nature of these systems demands new approaches that integrate technical safeguards with governance mechanisms. Both also emphasize the need for systemic, holistic design rather than treating security or alignment as an afterthought bolted onto deployed systems.

**Areas of Disagreement**

The first paper appears to focus primarily on *security*—specifically the emergent vulnerabilities that arise when AI agents interact, negotiate, or even deceive one another—framing the problem as one of adversarial dynamics and potential exploitation. The second paper, by contrast, takes a broader *governance and design* perspective, treating agentic AI as part of a sociotechnical ecosystem and emphasizing adaptive information, formal verification, and regulatory compliance as first-class design concerns. In short, the first treats multi-agent AI as a security problem to be hardened, while the second treats it as a governance and co-design problem to be continuously negotiated among stakeholders.

**Most Urgent Open Question**

What does a concrete, verifiable assurance framework look like for multi-agent AI systems that can simultaneously satisfy dynamic security requirements, adapt to evolving agent behaviors, and remain auditable by human regulators—without collapsing into either brittle over-constraint or unenforceable post-hoc governance?
